# Multi-Factor Authentication

<TagList tags={frontmatter.tags} />
<AttributionList contributors={frontmatter.contributors} />

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is one of the highest-leverage controls in operational security. It reduces account
compromise risk by requiring an additional factor beyond a password.

> 🔑 **Key Takeaway**: Make phishing-resistant MFA your default for high-value accounts. Hardware security keys and
> passkeys should be primary. OTP apps are fallback. SMS should be temporary and tightly scoped.

## MFA factor quality (practical hierarchy)

From strongest to weakest in most real-world threat models:

1. **Hardware-backed FIDO2/WebAuthn (security keys/passkeys on external authenticators)**
2. **Platform passkeys (device-bound passkeys with strong device security controls)**
3. **Authenticator app OTP (TOTP/HOTP)**
4. **SMS or voice OTP (legacy fallback only)**

## Implementation priorities

1. Protect accounts that can reset or cascade into others:
   - Primary email
   - SSO / identity provider
   - Password manager
   - Cloud, code hosting, and CI/CD administration
2. Remove weak fallback paths where possible.
3. Register at least two authenticators for each critical identity.
4. Test recovery flows to avoid lockout during incidents.

## Recommended baseline policy

- Require MFA for all organization members.
- Require phishing-resistant MFA for privileged roles.
- Enforce periodic review of enrolled authenticators.
- Maintain documented break-glass and recovery procedures.
- Alert on MFA reset, device enrollment, and recovery method changes.
