# Two-factor & hardware authentication

Strong authentication is the fastest way to reduce account takeover risk in high-impact operations.

## Prioritize phishing-resistant MFA

1. Prefer hardware security keys (FIDO2/WebAuthn) for all admin, treasury, exchange, registrar, and email accounts.
2. Use authenticator apps only when security keys are unavailable.
3. Avoid SMS-based verification on critical accounts because phone-number takeover (SIM swap) can bypass it.
4. Remove SMS fallback where possible.

## SIM-swapping risk controls

SIM swapping happens when an attacker convinces or coerces a carrier into transferring your phone number to a SIM they control.

### What to do now

1. Audit your highest-value accounts and disable SMS 2FA.
2. Move recovery and MFA methods to security keys and authenticator apps.
3. Use unique passwords and passkeys where supported.
4. Harden your email account first (it is often the root of account recovery).

### Carrier-side protections

1. Enable carrier account locks, PINs, and “no-port/no-SIM-change without in-person verification” options.
2. Request internal notes requiring extra verification before account changes.
3. Monitor for unexpected loss of service (a common SIM-swap signal) and have an emergency response path.

## Operational practices

1. Keep at least two security keys enrolled per critical service.
2. Store backup factors offline in a controlled location.
3. Review active sessions and account recovery settings on a regular cadence.
4. Test account-recovery paths quarterly so emergency actions are predictable.
