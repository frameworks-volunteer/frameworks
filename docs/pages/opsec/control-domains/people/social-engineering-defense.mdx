# Social engineering defense

Social engineering attacks target people first, then systems.

## Core defenses

1. Require verification for sensitive requests (fund movements, credential resets, key changes).
2. Use out-of-band confirmation for urgent or unusual requests.
3. Build a culture where staff can safely challenge suspicious instructions.
4. Keep reporting channels simple and fast.

## Training program

1. Deliver recurring role-based training (not one-time onboarding only).
2. Include phishing, vishing, impersonation, and pretexting scenarios.
3. Run realistic simulations and feed results into targeted coaching.

## Technical reinforcement

1. Apply email and messaging anti-phishing controls.
2. Restrict privileged actions through step-up authentication and approvals.
3. Log and review suspicious communications linked to account activity.

## Incident handling

1. Define response procedures for suspected social-engineering success.
2. Preserve evidence, revoke exposed access, and rotate secrets quickly.
3. Run post-incident reviews to improve playbooks and training.
