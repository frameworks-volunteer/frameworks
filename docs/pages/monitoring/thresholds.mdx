# Defining Thresholds for On-Chain Monitoring

<TagList tags={frontmatter.tags} />
<AttributionList contributors={frontmatter.contributors} />

The hardest part of monitoring is not collecting data. It is choosing thresholds that catch real incidents early without
creating constant alert fatigue.

## 1) Build a Baseline Before Choosing Numbers

Start with 2-6 weeks of normal behavior data (longer if your protocol is cyclical):

- Daily and hourly treasury transfer patterns
- Typical admin/owner transaction frequency
- Normal mint/burn and liquidation rates
- Node sync lag, RPC latency, and error-rate distributions

If you skip baseline work, your thresholds will either be too noisy or too blind.

## 2) Use Multi-Level Severity, Not One Hard Cutoff

For each signal, define at least two levels:

- **Warning**: requires triage during business hours
- **Critical**: requires immediate response/paging

This keeps low-confidence anomalies visible without burning out responders.

## 3) Concrete Threshold Examples (Starter Values)

These are examples to adapt, not universal constants.

### Treasury movement

- **Warning**: single transfer > 1% of treasury TVL
- **Critical**: single transfer > 5% of treasury TVL
- **Escalate faster if** destination is first-seen address or cross-chain bridge

### Privileged operations

- **Critical (always)**: `upgradeTo`, owner transfer, role-grant/revoke by unapproved signer
- **Warning**: privileged call outside normal maintenance window
- **Critical**: two or more privileged changes within 15 minutes

### Token integrity

- **Warning**: mint volume > 2x 7-day moving average
- **Critical**: mint/burn event from unauthorized role
- **Warning**: liquidation volume > 3 standard deviations over 30-day baseline

### Node and RPC health

- **Warning**: sync lag > 3 blocks for > 5 minutes
- **Critical**: sync lag > 10 blocks for > 3 minutes
- **Warning**: RPC error rate > 2% for 10 minutes
- **Critical**: RPC error rate > 5% for 5 minutes

## 4) Add Correlation Rules to Reduce Noise

High signal comes from combining conditions, for example:

- Large treasury outflow **and** signer set change in same 10-minute window
- Contract upgrade event **and** sudden spike in failed user transactions
- Bridge outflow anomaly **and** relayer/key-management incident

Correlated alerts should have higher severity than any single trigger alone.

## 5) Tune on a Weekly Cadence

Each week:

1. Review top noisy alerts by count and by responder feedback.
2. Tighten or relax thresholds with explicit change notes.
3. Add suppressions for known benign maintenance windows.
4. Validate that critical scenarios still trigger in simulations/tabletops.

Thresholds are living controls. Protocol growth, new chains, and new products should force threshold reviews.
